British Pageantry

Changing of The Guard

Also known as Guard Mounting, this historic ceremony dates back to 1660 and showcases the elite Foot Guards of the Household Division carrying out their solemn royal duty.

Ceremony Schedule

Guard Mounting takes place at Buckingham Palace on scheduled mornings throughout the year.

  • Days: Mon, Wed, Fri & Sun (Summer: Daily)
  • Forecourt Time: 11:00 AM sharp
  • Duration: Approx. 45 minutes
  • Weather Note: Cancelled in heavy rain

Best Vantage Points

Where to stand for the clearest views of the soldiers, horses, and regimental brass bands.

  • Victoria Memorial Steps: Elevated panoramic sightlines
  • Palace Forecourt Gates: Close-up view of the inspection
  • The Mall / Spur Road: Watch bands marching in formation
  • Friary Court (St James's): Less crowded inspection at 10:30 AM

Essential Visitor Advice

Crucial practical tips to make your experience comfortable and safe.

  • Arrival Time: Arrive by 10:15 AM (45 mins early)
  • Admission: 100% Free to watch from outside gates
  • Security: Be vigilant with phones and wallets in crowds
  • Restrooms: Public toilets available in St James's Park

Ceremony Timeline Breakdown

10:30 AM • ST JAMES'S PALACE

Inspection of the Old Guard Detachment

The Captain of the Queen's/King's Guard inspects the St James's Palace detachment at Friary Court before stepping off accompanied by the Corps of Drums or regimental band.

10:45 AM • WELLINGTON BARRACKS

The New Guard Departs

Formed on Birdcage Walk, the New Guard receives their Regimental Colours and marches proudly down Spur Road toward Buckingham Palace led by a full military brass band playing traditional marches and pop favorites.

11:00 AM • BUCKINGHAM PALACE FORECOURT

The Royal Handover Ceremony

The New Guard enters the North Gate into the palace forecourt. The Old Guard presents arms, and the Captains advance to symbolically hand over the Keys of the Palace.

11:15 AM - 11:35 AM • PARADE GROUND

Musical Performance & Sentry Relief

While the sentry boxes are relieved, the military band stands in the centre of the forecourt entertaining the gathered crowds with an eclectic repertoire ranging from classic military marches to West End musical anthems.

11:45 AM • DISMISSAL

Old Guard Marches Off

The relieved Old Guard marches out of the Centre Gates to return to Wellington Barracks. The New Guard takes up their posts for the next 24 or 48 hours.

Interior Splendour

The 19 State Rooms

Discover the opulent ceremonial rooms where monarchs have entertained global heads of state, held royal investitures, and hosted lavish banquets for over two centuries.

1. The Grand Staircase

Designed by architect John Nash for King George IV in the 1820s, featuring magnificent gilded bronze acanthus balustrades and an exquisite glass dome ceiling.

  • Architect: John Nash (1825)
  • Features: Gilded bronze floral casting, family portraits

2. The Throne Room

Draped in royal crimson damask, this ceremonial hall contains the single-step dais holding the original Chairs of Estate used at the coronations of Queen Elizabeth II and King Charles III.

  • Use: Royal addresses, wedding photo portraits
  • Ceiling: Frieze depicting the Wars of the Roses

3. The White Drawing Room

Perhaps the most famous room in the palace, decorated in white and gold French neo-classical splendour. It features a cunning secret door disguised as a mirror and console table!

  • Secret Door: Used by The King to enter directly from private apartments
  • Furnishings: English giltwood and Sèvres porcelain

4. The Grand Ballroom

Added by Queen Victoria in 1855, it is the largest room in the palace (36m long). It is the setting for State Banquets where up to 170 guests dine on historic silver-gilt services.

  • Capacity: 170 banquet seats; 1,000 investiture guests
  • First Event: Crimean War victory ball in 1856

5. The Picture Gallery

A 47-metre long gallery purpose-built to exhibit masterpieces from the world-renowned Royal Collection, naturally lit by continuous glazed roof lanterns.

  • Master Artists: Rembrandt, Vermeer, Titian, Rubens, Canaletto
  • Display: Artworks rotated periodically from royal reserves

6. The Music Room

With sixteen dramatic blue scagliola columns and an intricately inlaid parquet floor made of satinwood, tulipwood, and rosewood, this room has hosted royal christenings for generations.

  • Royal Christenings: King Charles III, Princess Anne, Prince William
  • Architecture: Large bay window overlooking the royal garden

Important Visitor Guidelines

Security & Bag Checks

Airport-style security checks apply before entry. Handbags and small rucksacks are permitted. Luggage larger than 45cm x 20cm x 30cm cannot be brought inside and there are NO left-luggage lockers on-site.

Photography Rules

Photography and video filming are strictly prohibited inside the State Rooms to protect the collection and visitors' experience. However, photography is fully permitted outside and in the Palace Gardens!

Accessibility & Buggies

The State Rooms are fully step-free accessible via lift. Wheelchairs and mobility scooters are welcome (booking in advance recommended). Baby strollers must be checked at the cloakroom; free baby carriers are provided.

London Navigation

Getting to The Palace

Buckingham Palace is located in the City of Westminster (SW1A 1AA) and is easily reached via London Underground, bus, or pleasant walks through royal parks.

Green Park Station

The closest step-free tube station to the Palace, approximately 6 minutes' walk through picturesque Green Park.

  • Piccadilly Line
  • Victoria Line
  • Jubilee Line
  • Step-free access: Yes (Lifts to street)

St James's Park Station

An 8-minute stroll along Petty France and through the scenic lakeside gardens of St James's Park with resident pelicans!

  • District Line
  • Circle Line
  • Scenery: St James's Park Lake & Duck Island

Victoria Station

Major mainline rail & tube interchange, 10 minutes' walk from the palace down Buckingham Palace Road.

  • National Rail: Trains to Gatwick Airport & South East
  • Victoria Coach Station: Long-distance UK coaches
  • London Buses: Routes 11, 211, C1, C10

Recommended 1-Day Royal Walking Route

The Classic Monarch's Walk (2.5 km • 35 mins walking)

Start at Trafalgar Square, pass under the historic Admiralty Arch, and stroll down the ceremonial red tarmac of The Mall. Arrive at the Queen Victoria Memorial and Buckingham Palace in time for Changing of the Guard at 11:00 AM. Afterwards, walk through St James's Park past the pelicans to Horse Guards Parade, emerge onto Whitehall past 10 Downing Street, and conclude your royal walk at Westminster Abbey and Big Ben!

Royal Trivia

Palace Secrets & Quiz

775 Rooms & 78 Bathrooms

The palace contains 19 State Rooms, 52 royal and guest bedrooms, 188 staff bedrooms, 92 offices, and 78 bathrooms. It also houses more than 760 windows and 1,514 doors!

Private Royal ATM

Deep in the basement of Buckingham Palace stands a private cash dispensing machine (ATM) installed by Coutts & Co., the prestigious private bank of the Royal Family.

350 Historic Clocks

The palace contains one of the largest collections of working horological treasures in the world. Two full-time Royal Horological Conservators wind and maintain them weekly.
